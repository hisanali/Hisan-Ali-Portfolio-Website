Who Actually
Owns Your Marketing?

The day you change agencies is the worst day to find out.

September 23, 202610 min read + auditBy Hisan Ali

The agency contract ended politely.
Then the logins stopped working.
Nobody had done anything wrong.

Access is a favour someone gives you. Ownership is control you keep. Own first, then grant access.

Picture a dental clinic in Muscat ending a three-year contract with its marketing agency. The handover meeting is friendly. The following week, the practice manager starts listing what the clinic actually controls, and the list is shorter than anyone expected.

The Google Ads account sits under the agency’s manager account, and nobody at the clinic has direct access. Instagram was set up with a former coordinator’s personal mobile number. The Analytics property lives inside the agency’s own Analytics account. The domain was registered by the freelancer who built the website in 2021, in their own name, and it renews in 19 days on their card.

This is a fictional teaching example. Nothing malicious happened. People created accounts with whatever email address and phone number were closest to hand, which is exactly why the problem is so common.

What you lose when someone else holds the keys

Losing a password is an inconvenience. Losing ownership can mean losing years of accumulated value that no new agency can rebuild quickly.

Domain

If it expires or is pointed elsewhere, your website and your business email stop working together.

Ad accounts

Campaign history, conversion data and audience lists that took years and real money to build.

Analytics

Your record of what worked. Without it, every new marketer starts from zero.

Maps and social

Reviews, followers and customer conversations: the public trust your customers can see.

None of this feels urgent on an ordinary Tuesday. All of it becomes urgent the moment a relationship changes: an agency leaves, an employee resigns, a freelancer stops answering, or a phone is lost.

Access is not ownership: the roles that matter

Every platform has a role that means “in control.” Your business should hold that role through accounts it controls, meaning a company email and a company phone number, with at least two trusted people able to get in. Agencies and freelancers should receive all the access they need to do excellent work, but not the master key.

Score your business in 30 minutes

Sit down with someone who can actually log in to each platform. Answer honestly for every asset. “Not sure” is a useful answer, because it tells you where to look first.

Take back control without starting a fight

Most ownership gaps are accidents of speed, not bad faith. Agencies often set accounts up quickly so that work can begin. Treat the fix as housekeeping rather than an accusation, and put the request in writing so that nothing is forgotten.

A POLITE OWNERSHIP REQUEST · SAMPLE

“We are tidying up the ownership of our marketing accounts so that the business holds the master access, while your team keeps everything it needs to keep working. Please could you add [company email] as Admin on our Google Ads account, transfer primary ownership of our Business Profile to it, and help us move our Analytics property into our own account? We would also like our Page, Instagram account, ad account and dataset in our own business portfolio, with your agency added as a partner. Could we agree a date this month?”

Then work in this order, because each step makes the next one easier:

  1. Domain and email first. Everything else depends on them. Confirm the registrant, the registrar login and the renewal date. ICANN’s guidance for registrants stresses keeping contact details accurate, because important notices about your domain arrive by email. [1]
  2. Customer-facing profiles next. Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp hold your reviews, followers and conversations. On Business Profile, only the primary owner can transfer primary ownership, and a new owner or manager must wait seven days before they can manage every feature. [9]
  3. Then ads and measurement. Add company Admins to Google Ads, take Admin of Tag Manager and sort out Analytics. Google Analytics can move a property from one account to another; plan it with your agency, because the person moving it needs the right roles. [10]
  4. Finally, clean up. Remove access for people who no longer need it, delete old Search Console verification tokens, and update recovery emails and phone numbers everywhere.

Keep it fixed: the offboarding rule

Ownership drifts. A new campaign gets set up in a hurry; a new employee opens a TikTok account on their own phone. Two habits prevent most future problems.

Keep a one-page asset register. For every asset, record the login email, who holds the master role, who else has access, the renewal date and who holds the two-step verification device. Review it every quarter and whenever someone joins or leaves.

Put ownership in the contract. A simple principle helps: accounts, data and creative made for the business are created in, or transferred to, the business’s name, and the agency receives the access it needs to deliver the work. Ask a lawyer to turn that principle into proper wording.

Same-day offboarding checklist

The best time to check is before you need to

An ownership audit is not exciting work. It is the kind of work that makes every future change easier: a new agency can start on day one, an employee can leave without taking your Instagram with them, and your measurement history survives every handover.

It also makes deeper work possible. You cannot run a holdout test or rebuild conversion tracking on accounts you cannot open.

Questions owners ask

Is it rude to ask my agency for admin access?

No. Clear ownership protects both sides: the business keeps its assets, and the agency is never blamed for problems it did not cause. A good agency can do its best work through the access you grant it.

Our Google Ads account sits under the agency’s manager account. Is it lost?

Not necessarily. Ask the agency to add a company email with Admin access directly on your account before anyone changes the manager-account link. Once you hold Admin, you can manage access yourself. [2]

What if the person who set up our Instagram has left?

First check whether the account is connected to a business portfolio and who holds full control there. If nobody at the business can get in, use Meta’s official recovery routes. This is exactly the situation two trusted admins are meant to prevent.

Does this matter if I work with a freelancer rather than an agency?

Yes, arguably more. Individuals change phones, email addresses and careers. The same rule applies: the business owns, and the freelancer gets access.

Audit method: each asset carries a weight from 2 to 5 based on how much damage its loss would cause, with the domain highest. The ownership score is the share of weight, across the assets you use, that your business holds. Fixes are ordered by weight, with assets held by others ranked ahead of uncertain ones.
