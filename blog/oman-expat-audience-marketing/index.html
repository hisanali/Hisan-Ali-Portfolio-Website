Marketing to Oman’s Expats:
2.36 Million Residents, Many Languages

Almost half of Oman’s residents are expatriates, and they are not one audience. The numbers, the communities, the languages, the dates that matter to them and how to plan campaigns that respect all of it.

Key takeaways
  • Nearly half of Oman’s residents are expatriates, so leaving them out of a plan often means leaving out half the market.
  • Bangladesh, India and Pakistan account for more than 80% of expatriate workers, but expats span every income level and life stage.
  • Language is the biggest lever. The same offer in Bengali, Urdu or Malayalam can reach people an English or Arabic ad never will.
  • Each community has its own calendar: Pohela Boishakh, Onam, Diwali, Pakistan Day, Christmas and more.

Walk through Ruwi on a Friday evening, Al Khuwair on a weekday lunchtime or a hypermarket in Barka at the start of the month, and you hear half a dozen languages in ten minutes. Yet a lot of marketing in Oman is still made in two: Arabic and English. That leaves a very large, very real audience hearing ads that were never really made for them.

This guide is about doing better, based on official numbers and on what I see working in campaigns. It is written with respect: expatriates in Oman are customers, neighbours and colleagues, not a “segment” to be targeted with the cheapest offer.

01The numbers

NCSI counted 2,359,816 expatriates in Oman in September 2026, 43.4% of the 5.43 million residents.1 The expatriate workforce is dominated by three countries. In the first quarter of 2025, NCSI data showed:2

India507,956

Together those three countries account for about 1.45 million workers, more than 80% of the expatriate workforce. The remaining expatriates include Egyptians, Filipinos, Sri Lankans, Jordanians, Sudanese, Europeans and many others, plus family members who are not in the workforce figures at all.

02Not one audience

Nationality is a useful starting point, but income, life stage and length of stay predict buying behaviour better. Four broad groups cover most expatriate customers in Oman:

Workers sending money home

Value, trust and connection

Phone plans, remittances, groceries, affordable clothing, travel home. Word of mouth and trust in the brand matter enormously; so does clarity on price.

Professional families

Schools, health and home

Schools, clinics, cars, furniture, restaurants and weekend activities. Often long-term residents with strong spending power.

Arab expatriates

Arabic, but not Omani Arabic

Egyptian, Levantine and Sudanese communities read Arabic but may not relate to heavy Omani dialect or Gulf-only cultural references.

Western & short-stay

English-first, research-heavy

Relocating professionals and their families search a lot, read reviews and rely on expat community groups for recommendations.

03Language decisions

The single most useful question for an expatriate campaign is not “which platform?” but “in which language will this person feel spoken to?”

Main languages to consider by community. Many professionals in every group are comfortable in English.
CommunityLanguages to considerNotes
BangladeshiBengali (Bangla)The largest expatriate group; Bengali creative is still rare, so it stands out.
IndianMalayalam, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, EnglishA very diverse community; Kerala has deep historic links with Oman, so Malayalam matters.
PakistaniUrdu, EnglishUrdu also reaches many Hindi speakers in spoken video.
FilipinoEnglish, TagalogEnglish works well; Tagalog phrases add warmth.
Arab expatriatesArabicUse a neutral tone; avoid dialect jokes that only land with Omanis.
Western & othersEnglishClear, factual copy; reviews and details matter.

Targeting tip: platform language targeting follows the language a person’s app is set to, and many expatriates keep their phones in English. Targeting “Bengali speakers” can miss most Bengali speakers. Combine language with location, interests and, where available, “lived in” country options, and let the creative’s language do the filtering.

Two practical points. First, video beats text across languages: a 20-second spoken Malayalam or Urdu clip with subtitles reaches people whose reading comfort varies. Second, translation is not localisation. Get a native speaker to write, not just check, the copy.

05Mistakes to avoid

Treating nationality as income

Every community in Oman includes doctors, engineers, business owners and workers. Segment by life stage and need, not by passport.

Machine translation

Automatic translation of slogans into Bengali or Malayalam often reads as careless or funny. Pay a native writer.

Forgetting Arabic

Signage and product information in Oman generally need Arabic. Add other languages alongside Arabic, not instead of it.

Only one channel

Many expatriate customers decide through WhatsApp groups and friends. Pair ads with a referral system and fast WhatsApp replies.

The brands that grow fastest in Oman are often the ones that make a customer feel, in their own language, that this business was made for them too.

Common questions

How many expatriates live in Oman?

NCSI counted 2,359,816 expatriates in September 2026, 43.4% of Oman’s 5.43 million residents.

Which nationalities are the largest expatriate groups in Oman?

Among expatriate workers, Bangladeshis are the largest group (622,078 in Q1 2025), followed by Indians (507,956) and Pakistanis (314,997). Together they make up more than 80% of the expatriate workforce.

Should I advertise in languages other than Arabic and English in Oman?

If a meaningful share of your customers speak another language at home, yes. Native-language creative in Bengali, Urdu, Malayalam or Hindi often stands out because few brands use it. Keep Arabic on signage and product information.

How do I target expatriates on Meta or TikTok?

Language settings alone under-count expatriates, because many use their apps in English. Combine location, interests and available “lived in” options, and let the language of the creative attract the right people.
