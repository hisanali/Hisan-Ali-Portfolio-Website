“Price in DM” Is
Costing You Customers

Hidden prices don’t filter out time-wasters. They filter out the buyers who won’t ask.

September 24, 20268 min read + calculatorBy Hisan Ali

“How much?”
“Price in DM 🙏”
Scroll. Gone.

The customers who ask are not your whole market. They are the ones patient enough to ask.

Picture a home-furniture shop in Muscat with a beautiful Instagram grid. Every post ends the same way: “Price in DM.” The team answers about 120 price questions a week. Most conversations stop right after the number is sent. The owner concludes that Instagram brings “time-wasters.”

But the inbox only shows people who asked. It cannot show the people who liked the sofa, wondered whether it was OMR 200 or OMR 2,000, and moved on without typing. Nor does it show the regular customer who asked at midnight, got a reply the next afternoon, and had already bought elsewhere.

This is a fictional teaching example, not a client case study. The pattern is common across social selling in the Gulf, and it is worth questioning with numbers rather than habit.

The four reasons behind “price in DM”, and what each misses

Hidden prices are rarely lazy. They usually come from a real worry. The problem is that each worry has a better answer than silence.

The logic behind hiding prices, and the gap in it
The reasonWhat it missesA better answer
“Competitors will copy our price.”Competitors can ask in a DM too. Only customers are really kept in the dark.Compete on what is included, not on secrecy.
“The price depends on the order.”Customers still need a starting point to judge whether to ask.Show a “from” price or a typical range.
“The DM starts a conversation.”It starts one with some buyers and ends it for others before it begins.Invite questions about size, delivery and options instead.
“A number looks expensive on its own.”Silence often looks more expensive than the real number.Show the price beside what justifies it.

What a hidden price really costs

The cost has three parts, and only one of them is visible in your inbox.

Staff time. Every price question needs a reply, often a follow-up, and sometimes a photo or two. Multiply a few minutes by hundreds of questions a month and the number is real, even for a small team.

Response delay. A customer who asks at 11 pm and gets an answer at 2 pm the next day has had fifteen hours to buy elsewhere. A price on the post answers instantly, at any hour. For the operating side of replying well, see the lead response system guide.

The customers who never ask. Some people will not message a business just to learn a price. They may be shy, busy, browsing at work, or simply unwilling to be followed up. You cannot count them in your inbox, which is exactly why they are easy to ignore.

What Oman’s consumer rules say about showing prices

Oman’s Consumer Protection Law and its Executive Regulation set clear expectations for price display. As summarised by the Times of Oman in June 2026, suppliers must show the selling price clearly on the product, its packaging or container, with Arabic as the required language, and restaurants, cafés and similar businesses must display a price list inclusive of applicable tax. Suppliers may not charge more than the announced or advertised price. [1] [2]

Those provisions are written with physical goods and premises in mind, and how they apply to a social media listing is a question for the Consumer Protection Authority, not a blog. [3] But the direction is plain: Oman expects prices to be clear before a sale, and a price published on a post also protects you, because it is on record when a customer later disputes what they were told.

Five better ways to show price without losing flexibility

01

Exact prices for standard items

If the product has one price, put it in the caption, on the image and in your catalogue. This is the simplest and most trusted option.

02

“From” prices for variants

“From OMR 145, depending on fabric” sets expectations while leaving room for options that change the final figure.

03

Typical ranges for projects

“Most three-seat sofas: OMR 220–380” helps project buyers judge fit without committing you to a quote.

04

Packages for services

Name two or three packages with what each includes. Customers compare packages much more easily than open-ended quotes.

The fifth option is to move the conversation to what genuinely needs a conversation. Replace “Price in DM” with a call to action that invites a useful question: “Message us your room size and we’ll suggest the right model.” The price stays public; the expertise becomes the reason to message.

Keep the price consistent across Instagram, WhatsApp catalogue, website and Google Business Profile. A different number on each channel creates exactly the doubt the price was meant to remove. The WhatsApp commerce guide covers catalogues in more detail.

Test it for a month before you decide

You do not need to believe this article. You can test it. Pick one product line, publish its price for four weeks, and keep everything else the same.

  1. Record a baseline. For the four weeks before, note price questions, conversations, orders and staff time for that product line.
  2. Publish the price. Add it to the caption, image, catalogue and website for that line only, using one of the formats above.
  3. Change the call to action. Invite questions about fit, delivery or options rather than price.
  4. Compare like with like. After four weeks, compare orders, conversations and staff hours. Watch for seasonal effects such as salary week, Eid or National Day, and compare with the same period last year if you can.

Before you post the next product

Let the price do the first conversation

A visible price is not a race to the bottom. It is a quiet salesperson that works at midnight, answers everyone at once and never forgets to reply. The DMs that remain are better ones: people who already know the price and want your help.

Questions shop owners ask

Won’t showing prices drive away customers who think we’re expensive?

Some, yes, and they would have left after the DM anyway, only later and after using your team’s time. Showing what is included beside the price helps the right customers see why it is worth it.

What if our prices change often?

Use “from” prices or ranges, date your price lists, and update the catalogue when prices change. Never charge more than the price you have advertised.

Does this apply to service businesses too?

Yes. Packages and typical ranges work well for salons, clinics, contractors and agencies. Customers rarely need an exact quote to decide whether to start a conversation.
