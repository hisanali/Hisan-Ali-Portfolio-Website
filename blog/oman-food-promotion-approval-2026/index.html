Promoting Food in Oman Now Needs Approval First.
What Brands, Restaurants and Influencers Should Do

Oman’s Food Safety and Quality Center has told companies, agencies and content creators to get its approval before promoting specified food products on social media, and has already summoned some for unapproved posts and health claims. Here is what the centre said, which claims cause trouble, and a quick check for your next post.

Updated Oct 31, 2026Warnings issued July–August 2026
October 31, 20266 min readBy Hisan Ali

Since late July, Oman’s Food Safety and Quality Center has been telling everyone who promotes food online the same thing: get approval first. The warning covers companies, advertising agencies, content creators and influencers alike. In August the centre summoned several of them over posts it had not approved and over health and nutrition claims it judged misleading.

In short
  • Approval is needed before advertising or promoting specified food products, on social media or any other media.
  • It applies to brands and influencers equally. Paying a creator does not move the responsibility.
  • Infant and toddler foods got their own reminder. Applications go to the centre by email.
  • The fastest way into trouble is a health or nutrition claim nobody approved.

Checked 31 October 2026. This summary is based on the centre’s public statements as reported by Omani press. The centre says the rule covers specified food products. Ask it whether your product is covered before you plan a campaign.

What the centre said

The Food Safety and Quality Center, which sits under the Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Water Resources, issued its warning at the end of July. It based the requirement on the Food Safety Law and Ministerial Decision 69/2024 on registering and promoting foodstuffs.

The centre has not published a new fine for this. Coverage of Decision 69/2024 in 2024 reported a fine of RO 1,000 per violation, doubled for a repeat. Treat that figure as a guide until you have checked the decision itself.

The claims that get posts pulled

Most food content is a photo, a price and a location. That is rarely what regulators object to. The risk sits in the caption, where a creator adds a benefit the product has never been approved to claim.

1

Health effects

“Boosts immunity”, “good for diabetics”, “detox”, “helps you sleep”. Claims about the body need evidence and approval. They are the first thing the centre listed.

2

Weight and nutrition

“Burns fat”, “zero sugar”, “high protein”. Nutrition claims must match the approved label. If the label does not say it, the caption should not either.

3

Babies and children

Infant and toddler foods have their own reminder. Any promotion of them needs approval, whoever posts it.

4

Unregistered imports

A product that is not registered for sale in Oman cannot be promoted into approval. Check registration before you sign a creator.

How to plan a campaign that passes

  1. Ask early. Email the centre with the product, its registration and the planned content before you book creators. Approval takes time, so add it to the campaign calendar.
  2. Write the caption first. Agree the exact claims with the brand, keep them to what the label and registration support, and send that wording for approval.
  3. Brief creators in writing. Give them the approved claims, the words to avoid and a rule: no unscripted health promises in voice-over or comments.
  4. Keep the paper trail. Save the approval, the brief and the final post. If the centre asks, you can show the post matched what was approved.
  5. Check the creator’s licence. Paid promotion in Oman already needs a licence from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion.

Show the food, name the ingredients, quote the label. Leave the miracles out.

Common questions

Do influencers in Oman need approval to promote food?

Yes. In July 2026 Oman’s Food Safety and Quality Center said influencers, content creators, agencies and food companies must obtain prior approval before advertising or promoting specified food products on social media or other media, citing the Food Safety Law and Ministerial Decision 69/2024.

How do you apply for food promotion approval in Oman?

The centre’s notice on infant and toddler foods directs applications to Food.approval@mafwr.gov.om. Send the product details, its registration and the planned content and claims, and allow time for review before the campaign starts.

What is the fine for promoting food without approval in Oman?

The 2026 statements refer to legal action under applicable law without naming a new figure. Coverage of Ministerial Decision 69/2024 in 2024 reported a fine of RO 1,000 per violation, doubled for repeat offences. Check the current text before relying on it.

Can a restaurant post photos of its dishes without approval?

The centre says the rule covers specified food products, and its enforcement focused on unapproved promotions and health or nutrition claims. Ordinary dish photos and prices are lower risk, but ask the centre if you also promote packaged products or make health claims.
