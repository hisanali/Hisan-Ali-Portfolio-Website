Oman’s New Rules for Marketing SMS and Calls:
8am to 9pm, “AD” Sender Names, Fines up to RO 30,000

In August the Telecommunications Regulatory Authority set new rules for promotional calls and messages. Marketing texts and calls may only go out between 8am and 9pm, promotional SMS must carry an “AD” sender ID with your real business name, personal numbers can no longer be used for selling, and every subscriber gets tools to block you. What the rules say, what they mean for your campaigns, and a checker for your next message.

Key takeaways
  • Timing: promotional calls and messages only between 8am and 9pm. A message sent in time but delivered late for technical reasons is exempt.
  • Identity: promotional SMS sender IDs must start with “AD” and reflect your legal or trade name. Promotional and service calls must show the caller’s name.
  • No personal lines for commercial calls and texts. A sales team texting from their own mobiles is now a problem.
  • Blocking: operators must let subscribers block all promotions or specific senders. Every irrelevant blast trains people to block you for good.

02What it looks like on a phone

For your customer, the change is visible in the inbox. Promotions arrive under an “AD” name they can recognise and block. Nothing promotional arrives at night. A text from an unknown personal number offering a deal is now, by definition, out of line.

Timing

Schedule for the window

Evening is often the best time for Oman retail texts. Under the rules, 9pm is a hard stop. Book your sends for 7 to 8pm, not 9:30pm.

Identity

One name, everywhere

Your AD sender ID, caller name, WhatsApp Business profile and shop sign should all say the same thing. Recognition is what keeps people from blocking you.

Teams

Move field sales and showroom staff onto business lines or WhatsApp Business accounts in your company’s name.

Lists

Blocking is permanent

Once a customer blocks your sender ID, every future SMS is gone, including useful ones. Send fewer, more relevant messages to people who asked for them.

03What is still unclear

  • Consent. Coverage stresses blocking and opt-outs but does not spell out prior-consent requirements for SMS. Oman’s personal data protection law already expects a lawful basis for marketing, so treat opt-in as the safe default.
  • WhatsApp and other apps. The rules regulate licensed telecom traffic: calls, SMS and short codes. WhatsApp marketing runs on Meta’s own rules and pricing. Respecting the same hours and naming there is still good practice.
  • The exact deadline. Some reports give six months to comply, which from mid-August points to about February 2027. Others give no date. Move now rather than wait for the cut-off.
  • Who gets fined. Penalties range from RO 500 to RO 30,000 depending on the breach. How liability splits between a brand and its SMS aggregator is not clear from the coverage. Check your provider contract.

04A compliant SMS setup

1

Register an AD sender ID in your trade name

Ask your SMS provider for an “AD” sender ID that matches your commercial registration name or trading brand. Keep transactional IDs, such as OTPs and delivery updates, separate.

2

Lock sending hours in the platform

Set a send window of 8am to 8:45pm so slow queues cannot push a blast past 9pm. Check scheduled campaigns that were set up before August.

3

Clean the list and record consent

Keep people who opted in, note where and when they did, and drop bought lists. Add “Reply STOP” and honour it within a day.

4

Move staff off personal numbers

Give sales staff business lines or WhatsApp Business accounts under the company name. Make it a written policy.

The rule says when you may send. Your customers decide whether you can ever send again.

Common questions

What time can businesses send promotional SMS in Oman?

Under the TRA regulation reported in August 2026, promotional calls and messages may only be made or sent between 8am and 9pm Oman time. A message sent inside that window but delayed in delivery for technical reasons is excepted.

What does the AD prefix on SMS sender IDs mean?

Promotional SMS in Oman must come from a sender ID that starts with “AD” and reflects the sender’s legal or commercial name, so recipients can see the message is an advertisement and who sent it.

Can sales staff send offers from their personal phones in Oman?

No. The TRA rules, as reported, ban the use of individual phone lines for commercial promotion. Businesses should use registered business lines, SMS platforms or business messaging accounts in the company’s name.

What are the fines for breaking Oman’s promotional message rules?

Reported penalties range from RO 500 to RO 30,000 depending on the violation, and double for a repeat offence within one year.
