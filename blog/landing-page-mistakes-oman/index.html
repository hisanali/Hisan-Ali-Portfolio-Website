Spot the Problems:
Why Your Landing Page Isn’t Converting

You paid for the click. The visitor arrived. Then they left without calling, booking or messaging. Most landing pages lose people for the same handful of reasons, and you can usually see them in seconds once you know where to look. Try finding them yourself first.

October 6, 20269 min read + spot-the-problems gameBy Hisan Ali
Key takeaways
  • Design for the phone first. Most visitors in Oman arrive on mobile, often from Instagram or Google.
  • The first screen must answer three things: what this is, who it is for and what to do next.
  • Match the ad. If the ad promised an offer, the page must show that offer immediately.
  • Remove friction: fewer form fields, a WhatsApp option and real proof near the button.

When campaigns underperform, the first instinct is to blame the ads: wrong audience, wrong budget, wrong platform. Often the ads are doing their job. They bring the right people to a page that then confuses, slows down or fails to reassure them.

The good news is that landing page problems are usually visible and fixable without a redesign. Below is a page for a fictional Muscat home-cleaning company. It has six common problems. See how many you can find.

Interactive
Spot the problems
Tap anything on the phone that would make you leave. Find all six, or reveal them and compare with the fixed page.

01The first screen decides everything

On a phone, the first screen is roughly what fits before the visitor scrolls. In those few seconds they need to understand three things without effort:

What

What is this?

A headline that says what you offer in plain words. “Deep home cleaning in Muscat” beats “Excellence redefined”.

Who

Is it for me?

Location, customer type or occasion. People need to see themselves in the page.

Next

What do I do?

One clear button, visible without scrolling. In Oman, a WhatsApp button often outperforms a form.

Match

Is this what I clicked?

The offer, image and wording should match the ad. A mismatch feels like a bait-and-switch.

The 5-second test: show your page to someone for five seconds on a phone, then hide it. Ask what the business does and what they were supposed to do. If they hesitate, your first screen needs work.

02Trust and proof, close to the button

People in Oman buy heavily on trust and recommendation. A stranger landing from an ad has neither, so the page must borrow it. Put proof near the place where they decide, not at the bottom of the page.

Real reviewsA short quote with a first name and area, or your Google rating with a link. Avoid generic testimonials that could be invented.
Real photosYour team, your work, your premises. Stock photos of smiling models make small businesses look less trustworthy, not more.
Clear pricesEven a “from OMR 25” range reduces anxiety and filters out people who were never going to buy.
A guaranteeWhat happens if something goes wrong? A simple promise answers the question people are afraid to ask.
Contact detailsA local number, a location or service area, and working hours. Hidden contact details feel suspicious.
Arabic and EnglishIf your customers speak both, offer both. A page in the wrong language is a page that isn’t read.

03Removing friction

Every extra step loses some people. Look at the journey from landing to enquiry and remove anything that is not essential.

FrictionWhy it hurtsFix
A slow pageVisitors on mobile data leave before it loadsCompress images, remove heavy sliders and unnecessary scripts
Long formsEach field feels like work and a privacy riskAsk only for name and phone; get the rest in the conversation
Pop-ups on arrivalThey block the content people came forRemove them, or delay until the visitor has scrolled
Several competing buttonsChoice creates hesitationOne main action, repeated down the page
Menu links to the full siteVisitors wander off and never returnKeep the landing page focused on one offer

04A 10-minute landing page check

Open your page on your own phone, on mobile data, and go through this list honestly:

1Does it load quickly on mobile data, not just office Wi-Fi?
2Can a stranger tell what you offer from the headline alone?
3Is the main button visible without scrolling?
4Does the page show the same offer the ad promised?
5Is there proof (reviews, photos, rating) near the button?
6Can someone contact you in one tap, by WhatsApp or phone?
7Are there prices or at least a price range?
8Is conversion tracking set up, so you know which ads bring enquiries?
“A landing page has one job. Every element either helps that job or gets in its way.”

GCCLanding pages across the GCC

Match the page language to the ad: Saudi and Kuwaiti campaigns usually need Arabic-first pages, the UAE often needs English with an Arabic version. Show prices in the local currency (AED, SAR, QAR, BHD, KWD), use a local WhatsApp number where possible, and remember the UAE’s Saturday–Sunday weekend when you schedule follow-ups.

Questions

What is a landing page?

A page built for one specific offer and one action, usually the destination of an ad. Unlike a homepage, it removes distractions so visitors focus on that single next step.

Should I send ads to my homepage or a landing page?

A focused landing page usually performs better, because it can match the ad exactly and remove menus and distractions. A homepage tries to serve everyone at once.

Is a WhatsApp button better than a form in Oman?

For many local businesses, yes. It feels personal and familiar. Keep a short form as an option for people who prefer it, and track both as conversions.

How do I know if my landing page is the problem?

If ads get clicks but few enquiries, and people leave quickly, the page is a likely cause. Conversion tracking and a few session recordings will show you where people drop off.
