“Made With AI” Is Now a Legal Label:
What the EU AI Act Asks of Marketers

Since 2 August 2026, the EU AI Act’s transparency rules apply. Deepfake-style images, audio and video must be disclosed, AI-written text on matters of public interest must be labelled, and the tools themselves must mark what they generate. Which of your content is caught, what a valid label looks like, and a decision tool that answers “do I need to label this?”

Updated Oct 28, 2026In force since 2 AugustMarking rule: 2 December for older tools

Key takeaways
  • If you publish AI images, audio or video that look real (deepfakes), you must disclose it visibly. A hidden watermark from the tool is not enough.
  • AI-written text needs a label when it informs the public on matters of public interest, unless a human exercised genuine editorial oversight.
  • People must be told when they are talking to a chatbot, unless it is obvious.
  • It applies to anyone deploying AI in the EU or reaching EU audiences. Platforms are adding their own labels too.

Checked 28 October 2026. This is a marketer’s summary of Article 50 of the EU AI Act and the Commission’s guidelines and FAQ, as reported by law firms. It is not legal advice; definitions such as “deepfake” and “public interest” are interpreted case by case. Ask a lawyer for anything regulated or political.

01The four duties

50(1)ChatbotsTell people they are interacting with AI, unless obvious.Provider
50(2)MarkingAI outputs must be marked in a machine-readable way (watermarks, metadata).Provider
50(3)Emotion & biometricsInform people exposed to emotion recognition or biometric categorisation.Deployer
50(4)Deepfakes & public-interest textDisclose realistic AI images, audio and video; label AI text that informs the public.Deployer: you

Marketers are mostly deployers: you use an AI tool, so the fourth duty is yours. The marking duty sits with the tool’s provider, with a grace period to 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market before 2 August.12

03Marketing cases

Label

AI model wearing your product

A photorealistic person who does not exist, in a real-looking setting, is a deepfake under the Act. Disclose it on the image.

Label

Cloned founder voice-over

AI audio imitating a real person must be disclosed, even with their consent.

Usually no

Illustrated or stylised visuals

Content nobody would mistake for a real photo or recording is generally outside the deepfake rule. Platforms may still ask for a label.

Depends

AI-drafted blog post

Commercial content is not “public interest”. Health or news-style content is, unless a human editor took real responsibility for it.

Label

Customer-service chatbot

Say it is a bot at the start of the conversation, unless it is obvious from context.

Keep

The tool’s watermark

Do not strip metadata or watermarks when exporting. The provider’s marking is part of the system.

Platforms are moving in the same direction: Meta, TikTok and YouTube already apply or require AI labels on realistic synthetic media. Google’s helpful content guidance now treats fake AI authors as deception.

04A labelling routine

1

Tag AI assets at creation

Add an “AI” field to your asset library. If it was generated or substantially altered, mark it there first.

2

Build the label into templates

A small, consistent badge for images and an opening-screen card for video. Decide the wording once.

3

Keep provider marks intact

Export settings that preserve metadata; no re-saving through tools that strip it.

4

Review public-interest text

Anything about health, money, safety or news gets a named human editor, or a label.

Disclosure is not an apology for using AI. It is what keeps the audience’s trust when they find out anyway.

Common questions

Do I have to label AI-generated content in the EU?

Since 2 August 2026, Article 50 of the EU AI Act requires deployers to disclose deepfakes (AI images, audio or video that appear real) and to label AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, unless there was genuine human editorial oversight. Providers must also mark AI outputs in a machine-readable way.

Is a watermark enough to comply with Article 50?

No, for deepfakes. The machine-readable marking is the provider’s duty; the deployer who publishes the content must also disclose it in a way people can clearly see, near the content itself.

Does old AI content need to be labelled?

No. The Commission says content generated before 2 August 2026 does not need to be labelled retroactively.

Do AI-written product descriptions need a label?

Generally no. The text labelling duty applies to text that informs the public on matters of public interest, such as news or health, not ordinary commercial copy. Platform rules may still apply.
