Social Media · Creator Marketing

Buy Outcomes,
Not Followers.

The creator with the biggest number is rarely the one your customers are watching.

September 23, 20269 min read + calculatorBy Hisan Ali

Creator A has 600,000 followers.
Creator B has 12,000.
Only one of those numbers is the right question.

A creator’s audience is only valuable to you where it overlaps with your customers: in place, language and need.

Picture a Muscat abaya boutique preparing a National Day collection. National Day falls on 20 and 21 November, so the owner wants creators introducing the collection in early November. [1] Two proposals arrive in the same week.

Creator A is a regional lifestyle creator with 600,000 followers, quoting OMR 1,500 for one Reel. Creator B is a Muscat-based fashion creator with 12,000 followers, quoting OMR 180 for two Reels. The team is leaning towards A. The number is bigger, and bigger feels safer.

This is a fictional teaching example, not a rate card or a market benchmark. Real quotes vary widely with the creator, the format and the rights you buy. The method is what matters.

Choose creators with evidence1. Followers versus local views2. Run the numbers3. Check the licence4. Brief for an outcome5. Measure what changed

Followers are potential. Views in Oman are delivery.

Before comparing prices, ask each creator for recent insights from comparable posts: average views or reach, and the countries and cities their audience comes from. In-app screenshots are fine, as long as they are recent and cover several posts rather than one lucky outlier.

In our example, the insights change the picture completely:

Views are not sales, of course. A creator can deliver cheap views to people who will never buy an abaya. That is why audience fit comes next, and why the numbers below end with sales rather than views.

Read the last row carefully. Creator A needs one sale for every 120 people in Oman who see the Reel. Creator B needs one sale for every 680. Neither figure predicts what will happen, but it shows how much harder the bigger deal has to work before it pays for itself.

Check the licence before you check the vibe

Oman regulates paid promotion on social media. Ministerial Decision 619/2022 from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion requires a licence to market and promote other businesses’ goods and services on websites and social media, and the rules have applied since 2023. [2] The official government service describes it as a licence for commercial companies to practise marketing and promotion on social media platforms and websites. [3]

Two points matter for any business hiring a creator. Licensed promoters are expected to display their licence number, and the decision requires those using a promoter’s services to verify that the promoter holds a licence. The decision provides for penalties including warnings, fines of up to OMR 1,000 and suspension or cancellation. Businesses promoting their own products are outside the requirement. [4]

In practice: ask for the licence number in your first message, check it against the creator’s profile, and record it in your agreement. If the creator is based in another GCC country, check whether their home market has its own rules as well.

Disclosure is also a platform rule. On Instagram and Facebook, branded content is published with a “Paid partnership” label that names the business partner. [5] TikTok asks creators to switch on its commercial content disclosure setting, and warns that undisclosed commercial content may not be eligible for the For You feed. [6] A clear label protects the creator, the brand and the audience’s trust.

Brief for an outcome, not a post

“Make a nice Reel about our collection” invites a nice Reel. A useful brief gives the creator one job, the facts they must get right, and the freedom to do it in their own voice.

ONE-PAGE CREATOR BRIEF · SAMPLE
Goal
One action: book a fitting on WhatsApp before 15 November.
Audience
Women in Muscat choosing outfits for National Day gatherings.
Message
The collection is designed in Oman, and alterations are ready in 48 hours.
Must include
The price range, booking dates, how to book and the Paid partnership label.
Freedom
Their language, whether Arabic, English or both, their format and their humour. Show the collection in the first seconds.
Respect
National symbols used with care, and no claim the boutique cannot prove.
Rights
Running the Reel as a partnership ad for 30 days, agreed and paid for separately.
Tracking
A personal link, code and WhatsApp keyword for each creator.

The “Respect” line is not decoration. Oman’s promotion rules ask licensees to respect national identity and cultural heritage, which deserves extra thought when a campaign borrows the colours and symbols of National Day. [4]

Agree paid amplification up front. Meta describes partnership ads as the moment a brand puts ad spend behind a creator’s content to reach people beyond the creator’s followers, and encourages creators to negotiate fairly for that extra usage. [5] Price it separately, with a clear duration.

Then ask the harder question: would these customers have bought anyway? Loyal customers often use a creator’s code for a purchase they were already planning. For paid amplification, the holdout test shows how to separate extra sales from sales that were already on their way.

Before you sign a creator

Pick the audience, then the creator

The best creator for your business is rarely the one with the largest number. It is the one whose audience already looks like your customers, who can explain your offer in a voice they trust, and whose price leaves room for the campaign to pay for itself.

Start with the customer you want, find the creators that customer already watches, and let the numbers, not the follower count, make the final call. For the path those customers take from discovery to a WhatsApp message, see the Oman buyer journey.

Questions before you pay a creator

How much should I pay an influencer in Oman?

There is no official rate card. Price the package against what it delivers: estimated views in Oman, content you can reuse and the usage rights you receive. Compare the cost per 1,000 Oman views with what you already pay for reach through ads.

Are smaller creators always the better deal?

No. Smaller local creators often deliver cheaper local views and a closer audience fit, but a larger creator can make sense for a launch that needs concentrated reach at one moment. Decide from the goal and the numbers, not from a rule.

Can we run the creator’s video as an ad?

Yes, with the creator’s permission. Partnership ads let a brand put spend behind a creator’s content to reach people beyond their followers. Agree the duration and the payment for those rights before the content is made. [5]
