About 19,000 Licensed Online Sellers in Oman.
Around 400 Are Verified. Here’s Why the Maroof Badge Matters

Oman’s commerce ministry runs a verification scheme for online stores through the Maroof Oman platform, and in July it offered to feature verified stores on its own social media. Few sellers have taken it up: roughly 400 verified stores against more than 19,000 licensed e-commerce activities. What verification is, how to get it, and how to turn trust into sales on Instagram and WhatsApp.

Updated Oct 31, 2026Figures to August 2026
October 31, 20266 min readBy Hisan Ali

Buying from an Instagram shop in Oman still involves a leap of faith: transfer first, hope the parcel arrives. The Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion has a tool aimed at exactly that doubt. Through the Maroof Oman platform, licensed sellers can verify their online store and display a certificate that shoppers can check. In July the ministry invited verified stores to send their certificates for a chance to be featured on its own social media channels.

In short
  • Licensing and verification are separate. E-commerce activity is licensed through the Oman Business Platform. Verification is done on Maroof Oman.
  • Verification ties your store and social accounts to your commercial registration, so shoppers can check who they are buying from.
  • Very few sellers have done it, so the badge still stands out.
  • The ministry offered free exposure on its channels to verified stores in July 2026.

Checked 31 October 2026. Counts come from August 2026 press reports, which differ slightly: 412 or 416 verified stores, and 19,260 or 19,366 licensed e-commerce activities. Sources also differ on whether verification is mandatory for every seller.

What verification is

Maroof Oman is the ministry’s platform for online store verification. A merchant creates an account with their Civil ID, adds business details, links the store to a commercial registration and lists the store’s website or social media accounts. After review, the store receives an official verification certificate, and shoppers can look up its legal status and details.

Why it can lift sales

Most Omani online shops sell through Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp, where a two-month-old fake shop and a ten-year-old business look the same. Trust signals that cannot be bought with followers are scarce, and verification is one of them.

1

Put it where the doubt is

Add “Verified e-store, Maroof Oman” and your CR number to your Instagram bio, WhatsApp Business profile, checkout page and order confirmations.

2

Answer the first question before it is asked

New customers ask whether you are real. Pin a highlight with the certificate, your address and your return policy.

3

Use it in ads

A line such as “Verified by the Ministry of Commerce” in paid social can lower the hesitation that costs you first orders. Keep the wording accurate.

4

Take the free exposure

Send your certificate to the ministry when it invites verified stores to be featured. A government account sharing your store is credibility you cannot buy.

How to get verified

  1. Licence first. Make sure the e-commerce activity is on your commercial registration through the Oman Business Platform.
  2. Create a merchant account on Maroof Oman with your Civil ID.
  3. Add business details and link your CR, then list your store website and social media accounts.
  4. Submit for review and keep the certificate when it is issued.
  5. Show it everywhere, and send it to the ministry when it calls for verified stores to feature.

Followers can be bought. A verified commercial registration cannot.

Common questions

What is Maroof Oman?

Maroof Oman is the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion’s platform for verifying online stores. Merchants link their store and social media accounts to their commercial registration and receive a verification certificate that shoppers can check.

How many online stores are verified in Oman?

August 2026 reports put the number of stores verified on Maroof Oman since February 2024 at 412 to 416, compared with about 19,300 licensed e-commerce activities.

Is Maroof verification mandatory in Oman?

Sources differ. A ministry statement describes verification on Maroof alongside licensing on the Oman Business Platform as required for e-commerce, while some market reports call it voluntary. Licensing is required; check with the ministry about verification for your business.

How do I verify my online store in Oman?

Create a merchant account on Maroof Oman with your Civil ID, add business details, link the store to your commercial registration, list your website or social accounts and submit for review. Approved stores receive an official certificate.
