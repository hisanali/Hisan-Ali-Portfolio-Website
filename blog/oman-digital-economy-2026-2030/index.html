Oman Wants Its Digital Economy to Add RO 1.5bn by 2030.
What Phase Two Means for Your Business

On 22 October Oman launched the second phase of its National Digital Economy Programme, running from 2026 to 2030. The targets: a RO 1.5 billion contribution to GDP, RO 300 million of technology investment and 3,000 direct jobs a year, with e-commerce, e-payments, SME digitisation and AI at the centre. What the plan says, where the opportunities are, and a readiness check for your business.

Updated Nov 3, 2026Launched 22 October 2026
November 3, 20266 min readBy Hisan Ali

Government programmes can feel far from the daily work of a shop or an agency. This one is closer than most. Its priorities are things small businesses already deal with: selling online, getting paid digitally, using software and, increasingly, AI. When a government sets targets in these areas, it brings support schemes, procurement and investment that follow them.

In short
  • The digital economy is about 9% of Oman’s GDP; Omanisation in the tech sector is around 72%.
  • Phase two focuses on e-commerce, e-payments, SME digitisation and AI.
  • The National E-commerce Plan is 87% complete, with 26 of 30 initiatives done.
  • For businesses: expect more support, competition and customer expectations online.

Common questions

What is phase two of Oman’s National Digital Economy Programme?

It is the 2026–2030 phase launched on 22 October 2026. It targets a RO 1.5 billion GDP contribution from the digital economy by 2030, RO 300 million of technology investment and 3,000 direct jobs a year.

What does the programme focus on?

E-commerce, electronic payments, digitising small and medium-sized businesses and artificial intelligence.

How big is Oman’s digital economy?

Reports on the launch put the digital economy at about 9% of GDP, with Omanisation in the technology sector at around 72%.

What should small businesses in Oman do?

Offer online ordering and digital payments, complete and verify online listings, keep a customer list, track sales by channel and adopt practical AI tools. These match the programme’s priorities and rising customer expectations.
