How Much Should You Spend on Marketing
in Oman? The 2026 Budget Guide

What global companies spend, what that means for an Omani business, how to split the money between ads, content, people and tools, and how to spread it across Oman’s uneven year.

Key takeaways
  • Large companies spend about 7.8% of revenue on marketing. Smaller and newer businesses usually need a higher share to grow.
  • Paid media is less than a third of a typical budget. The rest pays for the content, people and tools that make ads work.

“How much should we spend on marketing?” is the question I hear most from business owners in Muscat, usually followed by “What are others spending?” The honest answer has three parts: a percentage of revenue that fits your stage, a split between ads and everything that makes ads work, and a calendar that matches how demand actually moves in Oman.

01What others spend

The most widely quoted benchmark is Gartner’s annual CMO Spend Survey. In 2026, marketing budgets averaged 7.8% of company revenue, up slightly from 7.7% in 2025.1 The survey covers around 400 marketing leaders, mostly at large companies in North America and Europe.

Two other findings are more useful than the headline. In 2025, half of CMOs reported budgets of 6% of revenue or less, so the average is pulled up by big spenders. And paid media took only 30.6% of the marketing budget.2 In 2026, CMOs also allocated 15.3% of their budgets to AI initiatives.1

Why not just copy 7.8%? Big companies spend on top of strong brands and existing customers. A café in Al Mouj or a clinic in Sohar has less brand memory to lean on, so the share it needs, at least for the first years, is usually higher.

02Budget by stage

These ranges are rules of thumb from my work with businesses in Oman, not industry benchmarks. Use them as a starting point, then adjust with your own results.

12–20%Launch

First one to two years, or entering a new market. You are buying awareness and first customers.

8–12%Growth

Product works and customers return. Fund acquisition and brand together.

5–8%Steady

Established brand with strong repeat business. Defend share, invest in retention.

Margins matter too. A business with 70% gross margin, such as software or services, can afford a higher share than a supermarket with 15% margin. If marketing costs more than the gross profit it brings in, the percentage is wrong, whatever the benchmark says.

03How to split it

The most common mistake I see is putting almost the whole budget into ads, then running those ads with weak creative, a slow website and no tracking. For a small or medium business in Oman, this split is a sensible starting point:

04Spread it across the year

An equal monthly budget is easy to manage and almost always wrong in Oman. Demand clusters around Ramadan and Eid, back to school, Khareef and the November peak, and drops in the summer heat. Spending evenly means overspending in quiet months and running out of money when customers are actually buying.

A better approach is a small always-on base, usually 40–60% of the budget, plus peak pushes timed to your sector’s busy months. The Oman Marketing Year 2027 shows when those peaks fall next year.

05Prove it is working

A budget is a hypothesis. Three numbers tell you whether it is right:

Cost per customer

What does a new customer cost?

Total marketing spend in a period divided by new customers in that period. Compare it with what a customer is worth over a year.

Marketing efficiency

Revenue per OMR spent

New revenue divided by marketing spend. Track the trend month by month rather than chasing a single target.

Brand demand

Are more people searching for you?

Branded search shows whether spending builds memory, not just clicks. See do people search for your name?

Tracking

Can you see it at all?

Without conversion tracking, you are guessing. Start with conversion tracking for Oman businesses.

Common questions

What percentage of revenue should a business spend on marketing?

Gartner’s 2026 CMO Spend Survey found an average of 7.8% of revenue, mostly among large companies. Smaller and newer businesses often need more: around 12–20% at launch, 8–12% while growing and 5–8% once established, as a rule of thumb.

How should I split my marketing budget?

A sensible starting split for small and medium businesses is about 40% paid media, 25% content and creative, 25% people or agency support and 10% tools and tracking. Adjust it once you see what drives results.

Should I spend the same amount every month?

Rarely. In Oman, demand peaks around Ramadan and Eid, back to school, Khareef and November. Keep an always-on base and add extra budget before and during your sector’s peak months.

How do I know if my marketing budget is too low?

If your cost per customer is healthy and you still have customers you cannot reach, the budget is too low. If cost per customer keeps rising as you spend more, fix creative, targeting or your website before adding money.
