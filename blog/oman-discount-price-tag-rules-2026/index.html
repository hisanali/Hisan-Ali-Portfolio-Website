Before White Friday, Oman’s Ministry Reminded Shops:
Show the Old Price, the New Price and the Percentage

The Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion has reminded retailers how discounts must be shown: the original price, the discounted price and the discount percentage, side by side. Discounts must follow their licence and its dates, prices may not be raised before a sale, and breaking the rules can cost OMR 500, doubled for repeat offences. With White Friday on 27 November, a price-tag checker to get it right.

Updated Nov 12, 2026Ministry reminder, October 2026
November 12, 20265 min readBy Hisan Ali

Every November, some shops raise a price a week before a sale so the “discount” looks bigger. Oman’s rules are designed to stop exactly that. The ministry’s reminder, reported by Times of Oman in October, sets out what shoppers should see on every discounted item, and what shops need before they advertise a sale.

In short
  • Show the original price, discounted price and percentage clearly.
  • Get the licence before advertising a discount, display it in the shop and keep to its dates.
  • Don’t raise prices before a sale; changes to approved rates or dates need written approval.
  • In 2024 the ministry said many promotions only needed notice to the Consumer Protection Authority. Check which applies to your offer.

Common questions

How must discounts be displayed in Oman?

The ministry says retailers must clearly show the original price, the discounted price and the discount percentage.

Do I need a licence to advertise a discount in Oman?

The ministry’s October 2026 reminder says businesses must obtain a licence before advertising discounts and display it in store. Earlier 2024 guidance said many promotions only need notice to the Consumer Protection Authority, so confirm which applies to your offer.

What is the fine for breaking discount rules in Oman?

The ministry cited an administrative fine of OMR 500, doubled for repeat offences.

Can I raise a price before White Friday and then discount it?

No. The rules bar raising prices before applying a discount. The “original” price should be what the item really sold for.
