Meta Ads · Defaults Watch

Two Switches Meta Flipped This Week,
Inside Ads You Already Approved

On 28 October, Meta turned on “product browsing” in existing Facebook and Instagram ads. The same week, Threads became a default placement in every campaign. Neither needed your click. What each switch does, where the opt-outs really are, and an animated audit that tracks what you have checked.

Key takeaways
  • Product browsing adds a scrollable product carousel to your ad after someone taps it. It is now on in live ads unless you opted out via Meta’s notice before 28 October.
  • You can still turn it off per ad, in Advantage+ creative and destination settings. One report says it came back on when an ad was duplicated.
  • Threads is now a default placement in all campaigns. Reports say full exclusion was removed in August; you can only lower Threads bids with a placement value rule.
  • Both switches change where your money goes without changing your budget. Audit, then decide on purpose.

01Switch 1: product browsing

Product browsing lets people who tap your ad scroll through more of your catalogue inside Facebook or Instagram before going to your site. Meta tested it on new ads first; from 28 October it applies to existing ads too, citing a 3% average conversion gain from its own experiment, without defining the metric or the comparison.12

02Switch 2: Threads placement

Threads ads went global in April 2025 as a default for new campaigns, with an opt-out under manual placements. Agencies now report that Meta announced on 19 October a default inclusion in all campaigns worldwide, completed the week of 26 October, and that since August full exclusion is gone: a placement value rule can cut Threads bids by up to 90%, not to zero.34

Cheaper

Lower CPMs, reportedly

Agencies cite Threads CPMs 20–40% below Instagram feed. Cheap reach is still reach you did not plan.

Different

Conversation, not shopping

Threads is a text-first, discovery feed. Direct-response ads built for Reels may convert worse there.

Visible

Check the breakdown

In Ads Manager, break results down by placement. If Threads is spending and not converting, act.

Control

Bid down, not out

If reports hold, use a placement value rule to reduce Threads bids rather than expecting an exclusion box.

03Why defaults matter now

These are the latest in a run of on-by-default changes: AI creative enhancements, dynamic media, static-to-video, Advantage+ audiences. Each one is small. Together they decide what your ad looks like, where it runs and what you are responsible for. From 20 November, Meta’s new terms make you solely responsible for what its AI changes, including features switched on by default.

A default is a decision someone else made for you. Reviewing it is how you take the decision back.

04Decide, don’t drift

1

Leave it on for catalogue-heavy ads and off for single-offer ads, then compare cost per purchase after a week.

2

If you keep it, test text-led or conversational creative there instead of repurposed Reels.

Common questions

What is Meta product browsing in ads?

Product browsing adds a scrollable set of products from your catalogue that people see after tapping your Facebook or Instagram ad. From 28 October 2026 Meta switched it on in existing ads unless advertisers opted out beforehand. It can be turned off per ad in Advantage+ creative and destination settings.

Can I opt out of Threads ads placement?

Agencies report that since August 2026 advertisers cannot fully exclude Threads; they can only reduce Threads bids by up to 90% using a placement value rule. Threads became a default placement in all campaigns the week of 26 October 2026. Meta has not confirmed these details in a newsroom post.

Does product browsing improve results?

Meta cites a 3% average conversion gain from its own experiment but has not defined the metric or method. Test it: keep it on for some ads and off for others, and compare cost per purchase.

Why does product browsing turn back on?

One practitioner reported that the setting switched back on after duplicating an ad that had it off. Check the setting on any duplicated or newly created ads.
