Google’s 4 New Content Tests:
Effort, Originality, Skill and Accuracy

In October 2026, Google rewrote its “Creating helpful content” guidance. It now defines main content, names four attributes its quality raters judge, and calls fake author profiles deception. What changed, what it means for AI-assisted content, and a scorecard to test any page.

Key takeaways
  • Google now defines main content as any part of a page that directly helps it achieve its purpose, including tools, reviews, tabs and headings.
  • Quality raters judge four things: effort, originality, talent or skill, and accuracy, with a higher accuracy bar for topics that affect health, money or safety.
  • Fake authors (AI headshots, invented names, false credentials) are now named as deception.
  • AI use is not banned. Mass-produced AI text without human oversight is Google’s example of low effort.

01What changed

Google’s “Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content” page has been the main reference for content quality since 2022. The October 2026 rewrite does not announce a new algorithm. It makes the rules more specific, and in three places sharper.12

It landed in a busy month. Google’s September 2026 spam update rolled out from 24 September to 8 October, the longest spam update of the year, and site owners have been reporting Discover drops and Search Console delays since.3 Google has not confirmed a core update in October; the last one was in May.

02The four attributes

Effort

How much human work went into the content, or into the system that produced it. Original photos, testing, data collection and careful editing all show effort.

Low: AI text at scale, no review

Originality

Does the page add substantial value beyond its sources? New data, first-hand experience, analysis or a tool count. A rewritten summary, even with credits, does not.

Low: summaries of other pages

Talent or skill

Is it made with the skill the topic needs: clear writing, good design, real expertise, working code? Skill shows in the result, not in a bio.

Low: generic, error-filled, padded

Accuracy

Are the facts right and current? For health, money, legal and safety topics, content needs to be highly accurate and consistent with established expert consensus.

Low: outdated or invented facts

The new main content definition matters more than it looks. A calculator, comparison table or product review block is judged as part of the page’s quality, not as decoration. A broken or thin tool now pulls the page down; a genuinely useful one lifts it.4

Google’s guidance now says fabricated creator profiles, such as AI-generated headshots, made-up names or false credentials used to look like human experts, are deception, and that this makes a page untrustworthy to users and to its automated quality systems.5

On AI itself, the line is about oversight, not tools. Google’s example of little effort is using generative AI to create large numbers of texts without manual oversight and curation. AI-assisted content that is fact-checked, reviewed by a person and adds original value is not the target.6

The question is no longer “was AI used?” It is “would anyone notice if this page disappeared?”

04Fixes that move the score

Effort

Show your work

Add your own photos, screenshots, test results and the date you checked. Show how the content was made, briefly, near the top.

Originality

Add one thing nobody has

A small survey, a local price list, a calculator or a real client example turns a summary into a source others cite.

Skill

Edit like it’s print

Cut padding, answer the question early, use tables where readers compare. Make tools work on mobile; they are main content now.

Accuracy

Date and source facts

Link to primary sources, add a “checked on” date and set a review schedule. For health or money topics, get a qualified reviewer.

Authors

Real names only

Remove invented personas and stock or AI headshots. Use real people with an about page and links to work people can verify.

AI

Human in the loop

Use AI for drafts and research, then have a person fact-check, add experience and decide what is published. Do not publish at scale unreviewed.

Common questions

What changed in Google’s helpful content guidance in October 2026?

Google’s “Creating helpful content” page, dated 1 October 2026, now defines main content as any part of a page that helps it achieve its purpose, names four attributes quality raters assess (effort, originality, talent or skill, and accuracy), and calls fabricated author profiles deception.

Is AI-generated content against Google’s guidelines?

No. Google’s example of low effort is using generative AI to create large numbers of texts without manual oversight and curation. AI-assisted content that is reviewed, fact-checked and adds original value is not the problem.

Is the October 2026 change a Google ranking update?

No ranking update was announced. It is a documentation change. Rater attributes are used to evaluate Google’s systems, not as direct ranking signals, but they describe what those systems aim to reward.

What does Google mean by fake authors?

Fabricated creator profiles, such as AI-generated headshots, invented names or false credentials used to look like human experts. Google’s guidance calls this deception and says it makes a page untrustworthy.
